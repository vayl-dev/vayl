The architectural divide

A bag of facts vs a reconciling ledger

Both extract facts from a conversation with an LLM. What happens after a fact is stored is the entire difference — and it's the axis Mem0 optimised away from when they collapsed their write path to a single additive call.

The finding behind it

Reconciliation is order-dependent within a topic and independent across topics. Naive parallelism corrupts it (raced 4 of 6 runs in test); slot-affinity — one ordered queue per topic — is the only scheme that is both fast and correct. The window ≈ (busiest single topic) × latency, flat in total memory. That's what separates an architecture from a demo.

The real competitor

Mem0 is the additive laggard. The honest test is against a reconciling incumbent: Graphiti, the temporal knowledge-graph engine behind Zep. We ran both on the same model (gpt-4o), reading Graphiti's own valid/invalid edges. The result refuses an easy story — and that's the point.

Why retraction is structural, not a prompt bug

Graphiti reconciles by making a new edge contradict an old one. A retraction — "she left", "we dropped it" — asserts the absence of a relation and creates no contradicting positive edge, so its invalidation mis-fires or extracts nothing. Removal is only first-class if a fact's lifecycle is first-class. That's the honest edge: not "better memory," but correct forgetting — the thing audit, stale-cleanup, and GDPR erasure actually turn on.

Single run, self-hosted Graphiti on default config, gpt-4o, facts hand-adjudicated. Zep Cloud (their tuned product) may differ — to be confirmed before this is a claim.

Why it's defensible

Reconciliation is a systems problem, not a feature

We stress-tested the engine against deliberately adversarial input — scoped facts, sarcasm, hypotheticals, corrections, and domains it was never tuned for. Two findings explain why the gap is architectural, not a prompt away.

Finding 1 Extraction and reconciliation are one system

A reconciliation step bolted onto naive extraction doesn't generalize — on any domain it wasn't tuned for, it stored facts as "unknown" and blind-appended, becoming an additive bag again. We hit this, then fixed it: giving the LLM ownership of extraction (subject, value, scope) in the same call that reconciles makes it work on arbitrary facts — "my favourite colour is blue → actually green" now retires blue and returns green. The lesson is the moat: it's one coupled model, not a feature layered on a store — the part that compounds with your data.

Finding 2 A fact's lifecycle must be first-class

The one place a reconciling incumbent (Zep/Graphiti) measurably breaks is removal without replacement — 0/10 on our retraction battery. Its graph model has no positive edge to invalidate when a fact simply ends. Modelling the lifecycle explicitly — active → superseded → retracted, plus a confidence gate that refuses to delete on a "maybe" — is what closes it, and it's not a copyable prompt.

Everyone can supersede. Correct forgetting is the hard part — and the part that maps to audit and erasure.

Built for Europe

A jurisdiction the US incumbents can't relocate into

Mem0 and Zep are both US companies on US infrastructure. For European buyers that's not a preference — it's a data-transfer problem and, increasingly, a procurement blocker. It's also the one differentiator that is structural, not a feature race.

US SaaS · Mem0 / Zep

  • Data lands on US cloud by default — Schrems II transfer friction
  • An EU region under a US parent doesn't satisfy sovereignty buyers
  • Erasure is best-effort with no lineage to prove derived data is gone
  • No provenance trail to hand a regulator or an AI-Act auditor

Vayl · EU-sovereign

  • EU-hosted or in your own VPC — data never leaves the boundary
  • GDPR-native: right-to-erasure and right-of-access answered from the lineage graph, with a signed deletion certificate — and correct retraction (above) makes "forget this" real, not best-effort
  • Retention, TTL, and PII redaction as first-class policy
  • EU AI Act: provenance + confidence + audit log is the record-keeping and traceability the Act requires
Straight talk

Where the incumbents win, and who should pick which

Where Mem0 and Zep are genuinely strong

  • Mature, widely adopted, large integration & plugin ecosystems
  • Mem0: excellent extraction; Zep: a real temporal graph that reconciles supersession well
  • Published benchmarks and real production mileage
  • Fastest start if you want store & recall (Mem0) or an entity graph (Zep)

Choose Vayl when

  • Your agent acts on facts that change or get retracted — and must not act on the dead one
  • Correct forgetting matters: audit, stale-cleanup, GDPR erasure
  • European data residency or the AI Act is on your checklist
  • You want reconciliation at ~2 calls/fact, not ~15 calls + a graph DB

Mem0 accumulates. Zep supersedes. Vayl also forgets correctly — sovereign, provable, and current.
